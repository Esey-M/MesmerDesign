{{< answer >}}
**Apply one test to every stocking filler: will they still have it in March?** That rules out novelty gifts and rules in consumables, pocket-sized solvers, and small upgrades on things they use weekly. Most people spend $30–60 across four or five items.
{{< /answer >}}

Stockings are bought in a hurry and opened in ninety seconds, which is why so much of what goes in them is landfill by spring. The whole problem is that we buy for the *moment of opening* rather than for the year that follows.

One question fixes it. Will they still have this in March?

## Consumables always pass

{{< amazon-grid ids="gourmet-chocolate, lip-balm-set" >}}

Consumables can't clutter, which makes them structurally safe. **Good chocolate** — single origin, not the supermarket box — is the most reliable adult stocking filler there is.

Lip balm sounds boring until you notice it's the item people lose, replace, and are quietly pleased to receive.

## Small things that solve something

{{< amazon-grid ids="hand-warmers-rechargeable, phone-grip-stand, mini-travel-games" >}}

The **rechargeable hand warmer** is the standout. It doubles as a power bank, costs little, and gets used every cold walk, school run and touchline morning for years.

Mini travel games earn their place differently — they're for the flat hours between Christmas dinner and bed, when everyone is too full to move and bored of the television.

## For the person who gets one big thing too

{{< product-grid ids="christmas-calories-dont-count-mug, personalized-merry-christmas-mug" >}}

A mug is the right size to anchor a stocking rather than fill it — it takes up the volume so four small things feel like a full stocking rather than a handful.
