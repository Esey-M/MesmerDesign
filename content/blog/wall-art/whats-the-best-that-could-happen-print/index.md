## One word changed

"What's the worst that could happen?" is the question we all run automatically — before the
email, the pitch, the message, the leap. It's meant to be reassuring and it almost never is.
It just makes the downside vivid.

This print swaps one word, and the whole calculation changes. Ask what the *best* that could
happen is and your brain has to go and find an answer: the client says yes, the flat is
perfect, they say yes too. It's not a slogan about positivity. It's a small, stubborn
correction to where your attention goes by default — which is exactly the job a piece of wall
art above a desk should be doing.

## Bold enough to mean it

Rich maroon on warm cream, set in heavy condensed type that fills the frame. It reads from
across a room, which is the point — you're not meant to walk up and study it. It's dopamine
décor with the volume up: enough colour and weight to anchor a wall, simple enough that it
doesn't fight with anything else you've hung.

## How to print and frame it

Print on matte paper if you're doing it at home — matte hides fingerprints and glare, and it
suits the warm cream background far better than gloss. For a local print shop, ask for a heavy
matte or a lightly textured art paper.
