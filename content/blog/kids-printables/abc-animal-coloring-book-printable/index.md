## Why the colour guide changes everything

Most alphabet coloring books assume the child already knows the animal. That works fine for
Cat and Dog. It falls apart at Iguana, Narwhal, Quail and Yak — a four-year-old gets a shape
they can't name and colours it grey because nothing else occurs to them.

Putting a small colour picture in the corner of every page fixes that quietly. The child looks
up, sees a narwhal, learns a new word, and *then* picks up a pencil. And once they've seen it,
they're free to ignore it entirely and make the elephant pink — which is very much the point.
The guide is there to teach, not to grade.

## What each page is doing

Every page is quietly running four lessons at once, without ever feeling like a worksheet:

- **A big outlined letter** to colour — letter recognition, capital form
- **A friendly animal** drawn with bold, clean outlines — easy for small hands
- **The animal's name in hollow letters** at the bottom — tracing and first reading
- **A small colour guide picture** in the corner — vocabulary and real-world reference
