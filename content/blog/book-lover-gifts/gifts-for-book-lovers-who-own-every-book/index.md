{{< answer >}}
**Don't buy a reader a book — buy what surrounds the reading.** The safest bet is a rechargeable clip-on reading light with a warm setting: it solves the reading-in-bed problem every night, and almost nobody buys one for themselves.
{{< /answer >}}

Buying for a reader is a trap. They've curated their to-read list, they have opinions about
editions, and they've probably already read the thing you saw on a bestseller table. Guessing
against that is how you end up with a polite thank-you and a book that never moves off the
shelf.

So don't guess. Everything below is about the reading, not the books.

## The one that always lands

{{< amazon id="book-light-rechargeable" >}}

Warm light, clips to the cover, rechargeable rather than eating button batteries. It solves an
actual nightly negotiation between a reader and whoever they share a bed with. Of everything on
this list, this is the one that gets used most.

## For readers who leave the house

{{< amazon-grid ids="book-sleeve, kindle-ereader, reading-journal" >}}

A **padded book sleeve** is a small, thoughtful gift for anyone who carries a paperback in a
tote and has watched a cover slowly get destroyed by keys.

The **Kindle** needs judgment. It's excellent for commuters, travellers, and people who've
openly complained about shelf space — and mildly insulting to someone who loves physical books
and has told you so. Read the room.

## For the annotating reader

{{< amazon-grid ids="annotation-tabs, reading-pillow" >}}

Some readers write in their books; some find it barbaric. If yours is the first kind, sticky
tabs and no-bleed highlighters are a genuinely welcome, genuinely consumable gift.

The **reading wedge pillow** is for the person who reads in bed and complains about their neck.
It's not a glamorous gift, but it gets daily use — which is the whole point.

## For the shelf itself

{{< amazon id="bookends-decorative" >}}

Heavy bookends are one of those things everyone needs and nobody buys. They pair naturally with
art on the same shelf.

{{< product-grid ids="christmas-bookshop-puzzle, black-cat-king-poster" >}}

A bookshop jigsaw is an unusually good match for this person — cosy, slow, and shelf-adjacent.
And if their reading corner leans dark academia, the crowned black cat oil painting sits right
at home above a stack of books.

{{< product-grid ids="gratitude-journal-365-prompts" >}}

For readers who also write, a prompted journal is a gentler gift than a blank notebook — the
blank page is precisely what stops people starting.
