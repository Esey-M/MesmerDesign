{{< answer >}}
**Use 65 lb cardstock for kids' activity sheets, 110 lb for cards and party signs, and matte photo paper for printable wall art.** To make a worksheet reusable, laminate it or slide it into a dry-erase pocket and write with fine-tip dry erase markers — one sheet then lasts months.
{{< /answer >}}

A printable is a PDF. What it becomes is entirely down to what you print it on, and this is the
part nobody explains at checkout. A handwriting workbook printed on copy paper curls, tears, and
gets thrown away after one sitting. The same file on cardstock, laminated, gets used every
morning for a year.

Here's what to buy, by what you're making.

## Paper, by what you're printing

{{< amazon-grid ids="cardstock-white, cardstock-colored, photo-paper-matte" >}}

**Copy paper (20 lb)** — fine for one-off activity sheets and colouring pages you're happy to
recycle.

**65 lb cardstock** — the default for anything a child handles repeatedly. Stiff enough to hold
up, thin enough that a home printer won't complain.

**110 lb cardstock** — for greeting cards, party signage, place cards, and flashcards. This is
where a printable starts feeling like a bought product.

**Matte photo paper** — for printable wall art. Matte over gloss every time: it hides
fingerprints, doesn't glare under a lamp, and looks right behind glass.

## The reusable setup

This is the part that changes how much use you get out of a pack.

{{< amazon-grid ids="laminator, laminating-pouches, dry-erase-pockets, dry-erase-markers-fine" >}}

If you're laminating a whole workbook, a **thermal laminator** pays for itself quickly. Use 3
mil pouches for flexibility, 5 mil when a toddler is involved.

If you're doing a handful of pages, skip the machine entirely — **dry-erase pockets** do the
same job in two seconds and cost a fraction as much.

One detail that matters more than it should: use **fine-tip** dry erase markers. Chisel tips are
too fat to form letters properly on a handwriting sheet, and children get blamed for messy
writing that's really a marker problem.

## Keeping a pack together

{{< amazon-grid ids="binder-and-sleeves, paper-trimmer, kids-scissors-glue, colored-pencils" >}}

A binder with sheet protectors turns a stack of loose sheets into something that survives the
kitchen table. A **paper trimmer** is worth it the moment you're cutting flashcards or bunting —
scissors never come out square, and it shows.

For colouring, prefer **colored pencils** over markers. Markers bleed through cardstock and onto
whatever's underneath.

## Printing a lot?

{{< amazon id="printer-inkjet-color" >}}

If you print packs regularly, a tank-style printer costs more up front and dramatically less per
page than cartridges. If you print rarely, don't buy a printer — email the PDF to a local print
shop and ask for cardstock. It's usually cheaper than the ink.
