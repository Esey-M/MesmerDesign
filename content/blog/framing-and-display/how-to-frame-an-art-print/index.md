{{< answer >}}
**Buy a frame one size up from your print and use a mat** — an 8×10 print in an 11×14 matted frame looks dramatically better than the same print in a bare 8×10 frame, for a few dollars more. Hang it with the center at **57 inches** from the floor, and use damage-free strips if you're renting.

Our prints ship unframed, which is the honest way to sell art online — shipping glass is
expensive and it breaks. But it does mean the last step is yours, and it's the step that
decides whether the print looks like art or like a page.

Here's the short version of what actually matters.

## Match the frame to the print

Every print we sell comes in standard US sizes on purpose, so you can buy an off-the-shelf
frame rather than paying for custom framing.

If you're hanging more than one piece, a matching multi-pack is cheaper than buying frames
individually and guarantees the finishes actually match — mixing two "black wood" frames from
different brands rarely works.

## The mat is the upgrade

If you take one thing from this guide: a mat does more for a print than a more expensive frame
does. It creates a border of empty space that makes the eye treat the image as art, and it
holds the paper off the glass so the surface doesn't stick over time.

The easy version is to buy the frame one size up with a pre-cut mat — an 11×14 frame with an
8×10 opening, or a 16×20 frame with an 11×14 opening. You don't need to cut anything.

## Hanging it without wrecking the wall

{{< amazon-grid ids="hanging-strips, picture-hanging-kit, museum-putty" >}}

Damage-free strips are the renter's answer and they hold more than people expect. For anything
large or heavy, use a real hook — a 24×36 frame with glass coming off the wall is not a small
problem.

Two small things worth the money: a **level** so the frame is actually straight, and **museum
putty** on the bottom corners so it stays that way. Frames drift every time a door slams, and
straightening them forever is a genuinely annoying way to live.

{{< amazon id="poster-hanger-magnetic" >}}

If you'd rather not frame at all, magnetic wooden hangers grip the top and bottom edge and cost
a fraction of a frame. They suit prints you plan to swap seasonally.

## Prints worth framing properly

These are digital oil paintings with visible palette-knife texture, printed on museum-grade
matte paper. Matte is the right call for framing — it doesn't fight with the glass the way a
gloss finish does, and the brushwork reads properly under a mat.

For a custom pet portrait, the same rules apply, with one addition: go one size larger than you
think. Portraits reward scale.
