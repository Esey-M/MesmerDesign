{{< answer >}}
**Plain kraft paper, real ribbon, and a handwritten card will make almost any gift look considered** — for a few dollars, and with no wrapping skill. If folding paper defeats you, put the gift in a rigid box with tissue instead; it looks better than paper wrapped badly.
{{< /answer >}}

Nobody remembers the wrapping. They remember whether the gift felt thought about — and wrapping is the fastest signal of that, for or against.

You don't need skill. You need three things in a drawer.

## The year-round kit

{{< amazon-grid ids="gift-wrap-kraft, ribbon-twine, tissue-paper" >}}

**Kraft paper is the whole trick.** One roll covers birthdays, Christmas, and everything between, because plain brown reads as intentional rather than seasonal. Change the ribbon colour and the same paper feels festive in December and understated in June.

Patterned paper does the opposite — you buy four kinds, half-use each one, and every gift looks like it came from the same shop.

## If you can't fold

{{< amazon id="gift-boxes-kraft" >}}

A rigid box with tissue paper takes fifteen seconds and requires no technique. It's the right call for mugs, anything round, and anything with corners that will tear paper.

This matters more than it sounds for our own products — a mug wrapped in paper always ends up looking like a mug wrapped in paper.

## The part that actually lands

{{< amazon id="blank-cards" >}}

Here's the uncomfortable truth about gifting: **the card outlives the gift.** People throw away the sweatshirt eventually. They keep the note in a drawer for fifteen years.

Buy a box of blank cards once and stop buying single cards at checkout for four dollars each. Then write more than your name in it.

## Wrapping a print

Rolled prints need a tube or a flat mailer, not paper. If you're giving art, consider framing it first — our [framing guide](/blog/framing-and-display/how-to-frame-an-art-print/) covers what size frame each print needs, and a framed piece needs no wrapping beyond tissue and ribbon.
