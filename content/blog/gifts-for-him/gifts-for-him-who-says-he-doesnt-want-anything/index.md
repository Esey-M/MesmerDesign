{{< answer >}}
**Upgrade something he already uses daily instead of giving him a new object.** Men who say they want nothing are usually objecting to clutter, not to gifts — so a better coffee setup, a tray for the pocket-dump, or a tool he'll use weekly gets past the objection where a novelty gift won't.

{{< /answer >}}

"Don't get me anything" is rarely modesty. It's usually a man who has looked around his house, counted the objects he doesn't use, and decided not to add more.

Take him at his word — then ignore the letter of it. Don't add an object. Improve one he already has.

## Upgrade the coffee

He already drinks coffee every morning. The mug is the cheap half of that ritual; the brewing is where a gift actually changes his day.

A pour-over setup is the rare gift that's both useful and a little ceremonial. It takes four minutes instead of thirty seconds, which sounds like a downside until you realise those four minutes are the point.

## For a new desk

Starting a new job is the one window where a man will happily accept small practical things — because he's suddenly aware of everything his desk doesn't have.

{{< amazon-grid ids="leather-valet-tray, wireless-charging-stand" >}}

The valet tray solves a problem he complains about without ever fixing: keys, wallet, watch and coins ending up in four different places every evening.

## Things he'll actually reach for

{{< amazon-grid ids="multi-tool-pocket, whiskey-glass-set" >}}

The multi-tool is the classic answer to this whole question. He'll say it's unnecessary and then use it within the week — a box, a loose screw, a splinter.

Whiskey glasses work on a different logic: they get used the evening they're opened, which makes the gift feel immediately worth it rather than filed away for later.

## If humour is the right register

One rule with funny gifts: the joke should be one **he** would make, not one about him. A shirt about how much he loves his dog lands. A shirt teasing him for something he's sensitive about does not, however funny it seemed in the shop.
